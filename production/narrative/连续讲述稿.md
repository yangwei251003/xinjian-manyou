## attention-limits

01　为什么眼前的信息，也会被漏掉？

Why can we miss something right in front of us?

02　想象你正专心追踪画面里的白色圆点。

Imagine carefully following the white dots on screen.

03　同一画面还有别的变化，但你的任务没有要求寻找它。

Other things also change, but your task does not ask you to find them.

04　问题不只是眼睛收到了多少信息，还有哪些信息得到注意。

The question is not only what reaches your eyes, but what receives attention.

05　选择性注意，就是让部分信息获得处理上的优先。

Selective attention gives some information priority in processing.

06　当前目标，会影响哪些对象更容易被选中。

Your current goal affects which objects are more likely to be selected.

07　被选中的细节更突出，其他细节却可能没有进入报告。

Selected details stand out; others may never enter your report.

08　出现在视野里、被注意到和被报告，是不同环节。

Being in view, noticing something and reporting it are different steps.

09　1999年，Simons与Chabris让参与者观看传球视频。

In 1999, Simons and Chabris asked participants to watch passing players.

10　参与者带着计数任务，关注指定队伍的传球。

Participants counted passes made by a specified team.

11　一些人没有报告画面中出现的意外角色。

Some did not report an unexpected character appearing in the scene.

12　这种任务中的漏看，被称为不注意盲视。

Missing an unexpected event in this task is called inattentional blindness.

13　研究提醒我们：物体出现在视野里，不保证被注意到。

The finding reminds us that being in view does not guarantee attention.

14　但不同任务、对象和观看条件，会影响遗漏发生的情况。

Different tasks, objects and viewing conditions can change what is missed.

15　所以，不能把一次漏看解释成一个人永远不细心。

One missed event cannot establish that someone is always careless.

16　更有用的问题是：他当时究竟在找什么？

A more useful question is: what was the person looking for?

17　记笔记时，你可能更关注板书，漏掉老师的一次手势。

While taking notes, you may focus on what is written on the board and miss the teacher's gesture.

18　寻找朋友时，你可能盯着衣服颜色，忽略别的位置。

While finding a friend, you may focus on clothing and overlook another location.

19　这两个例子都说明，目标会改变信息的优先顺序。

Both examples show how goals can change information priorities.

20　需要检查细节时，可以先把观察目标说清楚。

When details matter, first make the observation goal explicit.

21　然后换一个目标再看，核对先前没有留意的部分。

Then look with another goal and check what you overlooked.

22　重新观察不能保证发现一切，却能增加一次核对机会。

Looking again cannot guarantee that you will find everything, but it gives you another chance to check.

23　注意力帮助我们选择，也意味着选择之外可能有遗漏。

Attention helps us select, and selection can leave things out.

24　下次觉得“这么明显怎么没看到”，先问一句：当时在看什么？

Before asking why someone missed the obvious, ask what they were attending to.

## memory-calibration

01　记得很清楚，为什么还要核对？

Why check a memory that feels perfectly clear?

02　回想一次活动时，你找回的并不是一段完整录像。

Recalling an event is not replaying a complete recording.

03　经历当时，注意、理解和已有知识已经影响了编码。

Attention, understanding and prior knowledge already shaped its encoding.

04　因此，最初留下的线索就可能并不完整。

The original memory traces may therefore be incomplete.

05　回忆时，我们会把这些线索重新组织成一个故事。

At recall, we reorganize those traces into a story.

06　后来听到的话、看到的照片，也可能参与这个过程。

Later comments and photographs can also enter that process.

07　于是，亲眼见到和后来听说的内容，有时会混在一起。

What we witnessed and what we later heard can sometimes become mixed.

08　这就是为什么回忆会重建，而不只是原样取出。

This is why remembering involves reconstruction, not simple retrieval.

09　1974年，Loftus与Palmer研究了提问措辞与事故回忆。

In 1974, Loftus and Palmer studied wording and memory for an accident.

10　一项实验中，用更强烈的碰撞动词提问，车速估计更高。

In one experiment, stronger collision wording led to higher speed estimates.

11　另一项实验中，一周后也有人报告了影片中没有的碎玻璃。

In another experiment, some reported broken glass a week later, although none was shown.

12　这说明后来信息可以影响回忆，不代表每段记忆都虚假。

Later information can affect recall; it does not make every memory false.

13　接下来还有一个问题：我们怎样判断自己记得是否准确？

There is another question: how do we judge the accuracy of our memory?

14　这种对自己认知的判断，属于元认知。

Judging our own cognition is part of metacognition.

15　清晰和熟悉是一种感觉，准确则需要与事实核对。

Clarity and familiarity are feelings; accuracy requires checking against facts.

16　两者可能一致，也可能出现偏差，不能直接画等号。

They may agree or diverge, so they cannot simply be equated.

17　比如你确信朋友说过一句话，却想不起当时的原话。

You may feel sure a friend said something, yet not recall the exact words.

18　这时先区分：哪些是记得的，哪些是自己的推断。

First separate what you remember from what you inferred.

19　有记录时可以核对；没有记录时，也可以保留不确定。

Check a record if available, or leave room for uncertainty if it is not.

20　把判断与核对结果比较，才能逐渐校准信心。

Comparing judgments with checks helps calibrate confidence over time.

21　这种核对不是要求怀疑一切，而是让确定程度有依据。

Checking does not mean doubting everything; it gives confidence a basis.

22　尤其在重要分歧中，确信的语气不能代替共同证据。

In an important disagreement, certainty of tone cannot replace shared evidence.

23　记忆重建回答“内容怎样形成”，元认知回答“我怎样判断它”。

Reconstruction concerns how recall forms; metacognition concerns how we judge it.

24　把两者分开，你就能认真记得，也容许自己修正。

Distinguish them, and you can value memory while allowing correction.

## learning-loop

01　看了三遍，为什么合上书还是说不出？

Why can three readings still leave you unable to explain?

02　材料在眼前时，你可以借助它顺利跟上。

With the material visible, it can help you follow along.

03　但跟得上，不等于离开材料以后仍能想起。

Following along does not guarantee recall without it.

04　要检查保持得怎样，就需要一次独立的尝试。

An independent attempt is needed to check what remains.

05　主动提取，是先从记忆中找答案，而不是立刻重看。

Retrieval means first recalling an answer instead of immediately rereading.

06　2006年，Roediger与Karpicke比较再学习和记忆测验。

In 2006, Roediger and Karpicke compared restudy with memory testing.

07　参与者学习短文后，在不同条件下再读或尝试回忆。

After studying passages, participants restudied or tried to recall them.

08　研究还把最终测验安排在不同时间。

The final tests were also given after different delays.

09　五分钟后的测验，再学习表现较好。

On the five-minute test, restudying performed better.

10　两天或一周后，先前提取的保持表现更好。

After two days or one week, prior retrieval produced better retention.

11　关键不是“阅读没用”，而是即时顺利不等于长期保持。

The lesson is not that reading is useless, but that ease is not lasting retention.

12　因此，复习可以加入合上材料、自己解释的环节。

Revision can therefore include closing the material and explaining it yourself.

13　发现缺口后再核对，让反馈指出需要补的部分。

Then check the gaps, using feedback to identify what needs work.

14　不过，知道一种方法，还不等于每次都能开始。

Knowing a method, however, does not ensure that you will start it.

15　习惯研究关注另一层：稳定情境怎样帮助动作启动。

Habit research concerns another level: how stable contexts cue action.

16　例如把一次简短自测，安排在固定的学习收尾处。

For example, place a brief self-test at a consistent point after studying.

17　这是安排练习的建议，不是前面那项实验验证的完整流程。

This is a planning suggestion, not the complete procedure tested in that study.

18　还要给睡眠留位置：睡眠参与恢复与记忆相关过程。

Also leave room for sleep, which supports restoration and memory-related processes.

19　睡眠不能代替清醒时的学习，自测也不能代替休息。

Sleep cannot replace waking study, and self-testing cannot replace rest.

20　这三类知识分别回答：怎样练、怎样开始、怎样安排休息。

These ideas address how to practise, how to begin, and how to allow rest.

21　把它们用于计划时，仍要按任务和自己的情况调整。

A plan combining them still needs adjustment for the task and your circumstances.

22　下一次复习，试着先回答一个问题，再核对一次。

Next time, try answering one question before checking it.

23　真正要观察的，是过一段时间后还能不能解释。

What matters is whether you can still explain it after a delay.

24　从“看过”到“想起”，让练习、反馈和生活安排各司其职。

From exposure to recall, let practice, feedback and daily routines play distinct roles.

## emotion-context

01　同样心跳快，为什么有时期待，有时紧张？

Why can a racing heart feel exciting at one time and tense at another?

02　身体变化提供线索，却不会单独写好情绪的全部含义。

Bodily changes provide clues, not the full meaning of an emotion.

03　发生了什么、你在意什么，以及怎样理解，都值得一起看。

Consider what is happening, what matters to you, and how you interpret it.

04　比如同样站在台前，有人想到展示，有人想到被评价。

On the same stage, one person may think of sharing and another of judgment.

05　所以，辨认情绪可以从身体信号开始，再放回情境。

To understand an emotion, notice bodily signals and then their context.

06　这是一种理解线索的方式，不是从心跳直接诊断心理状态。

This is a way to interpret clues, not to diagnose a state from heart rate.

07　如果任务要求与你觉得可用的资源发生冲突，压力也会进入。

Stress may arise when demands conflict with the resources you perceive.

08　这里的资源，可以是时间、准备、协作和支持。

Resources may include time, preparation, cooperation and support.

09　因此，不舒服既可能涉及解释，也可能涉及真实的负荷。

Discomfort may involve both interpretation and real demands.

10　只改想法，却不看任务和资源，可能漏掉需要解决的问题。

Changing thoughts alone can overlook demands that need practical solutions.

11　认知重评关注其中一环：能否换一种符合证据的解释。

Reappraisal addresses one part: finding another interpretation consistent with evidence.

12　比如上台心跳加快，不一定意味着这次一定会失败。

For example, a racing heart on stage does not mean that this attempt will fail.

13　可以试着想：我虽紧张，仍能按准备的步骤开始。

One possible reappraisal is: I can feel tense and still begin with the steps I prepared.

14　研究提示重评有时能改变情绪反应，但效果依情境而异。

Research suggests reappraisal can affect emotional responses, with contextual limits.

15　它既不是万能开关，也不要求你把坏事说成好事。

It is neither a universal switch nor a demand to call bad events good.

16　于是，可以把眼前的问题分成两条需要核对的线。

You can therefore examine the situation along two lines.

17　一条问：我怎样理解这件事，还有哪些合理解释？

One asks how you interpret it and what other explanations are reasonable.

18　另一条问：负荷是否过大，时间、分工或支持能否调整？

The other asks whether demands, time, workload or support can change.

19　两条线可以同时处理，不必争论究竟全是想法还是全是环境。

Both can matter; neither thoughts nor circumstances must explain everything.

20　行动也因此更具体：核对事实、重新安排，或找人商量。

Action becomes more specific: check facts, reorganize, or talk with someone.

21　如果困扰持续影响学习和生活，可以寻求可靠成人与专业支持。

If distress keeps affecting daily life or learning, seek trusted adult and professional support.

22　一次情绪体验，不能概括一个完整的人。

One emotional experience cannot define a whole person.

23　身体、解释与资源是不同线索，分开看，才能一起理解。

Body, interpretation and resources are distinct clues that can be considered together.

24　先知道哪一环需要帮助，再选择下一步。

Identify which part needs support, then choose a next step.

## comparison-personality

01　别人的高光，能说明你是什么样的人吗？

Can another person's highlight define who you are?

02　看到同学获奖，你看到的是一个结果，不是全部过程。

A classmate's award shows an outcome, not the entire process.

03　你拿来比较的自己，却可能包含失败、疲惫和每次犹豫。

You may compare that highlight with all your own failures, fatigue and hesitation.

04　两边的信息不对称，比较就很容易失去同一把尺。

With unequal information, the comparison can lose a common basis.

05　社会比较关注我们怎样借助他人评价自己。

Social comparison concerns how we evaluate ourselves in relation to others.

06　比较对象、维度和对差距的解释，都会影响这段过程。

The person, dimension and interpretation of a difference all matter.

07　别人表达流利，并不能直接推出“我天生不擅长表达”。

Someone else's fluent speaking does not establish that you are inherently bad at it.

08　因为一次表现，还不足以概括一个人的稳定倾向。

A single performance is not enough to describe a person's tendencies.

09　人格特质描述的是相对稳定的差异，常用连续维度表达。

Personality traits describe relatively stable differences, often along continuous dimensions.

10　它们不是把所有人塞进几个互不相通的盒子。

They do not sort everyone into a few sealed, separate boxes.

11　同一个人在课堂、熟悉朋友面前和陌生环境里，也可能表现不同。

The same person can behave differently in class, with friends, and in unfamiliar settings.

12　所以，理解倾向需要多次观察，也要交代情境。

Understanding a tendency needs repeated observations and attention to context.

13　这就把两个知识点连起来：比较不能代替完整的人格理解。

This connects the ideas: comparison cannot replace a fuller understanding of personality.

14　别人的一个亮点，也不能给你的全部能力定性。

One strength in another person cannot define all your abilities.

15　如果比较带来困扰，先问：我比较的究竟是哪一项？

If a comparison troubles you, first ask exactly what dimension you are comparing.

16　再问：练习、时间、机会和支持，有哪些条件不同？

Then ask how practice, time, opportunity and support differ.

17　把这些条件补回来，差距就不再只剩一个笼统标签。

Adding those conditions makes the difference more than a vague label.

18　接着，从别人那里找一种自己能尝试的方法。

Next, identify one method from the other person that you could try.

19　例如学习怎样准备一次发言，而不是证明谁更有价值。

For example, learn how to prepare a talk instead of ranking personal worth.

20　再用具体行为观察自己的变化。

Then observe changes through specific actions.

21　这种方法不保证所有比较都会舒服，也不等于特质毫无意义。

This does not make every comparison comfortable or make traits meaningless.

22　它只是让比较的范围，与证据真正能说明的范围一致。

It keeps the scope of the comparison within what the evidence supports.

23　看清一项差异，和判定一个完整的人，是两件事。

Understanding one difference is distinct from judging a whole person.

24　带走一种方法，比带走一个限制自己的标签更可操作。

Taking away a method gives you more to act on than a limiting label.

## support-exploration

01　需要帮助，会让人更不独立吗？

Does needing help make someone less independent?

02　先分清两件事：得到支持，与把所有决定交给别人，并不相同。

Receiving support is different from handing all decisions to someone else.

03　支持可以提供资源，而行动和选择仍有自己的位置。

Support can provide resources while leaving room for your own action and choice.

04　要理解这个关系，可以先看依恋研究提出的问题。

Attachment research offers one starting question about this relationship.

05　在婴幼儿研究中，研究者观察照顾者的回应与儿童的探索。

Researchers have observed caregiver responses and exploration in young children.

06　安全基地描述一种支持探索的关系功能。

A secure base describes a relationship that supports exploration.

07　这个概念帮助理解，靠近支持与继续探索可以并存。

The concept helps explain how seeking support and exploring can coexist.

08　但婴幼儿情境中的观察，不能直接给青少年的关系分型。

Observations of infants cannot directly classify an adolescent's relationships.

09　回到青少年生活，我们要另看适合这个年龄的支持资料。

For adolescents, we need guidance appropriate to that age group.

10　WHO强调家庭、学校与社区中的支持性环境。

WHO emphasizes supportive environments in families, schools and communities.

11　同伴可以倾听和陪伴，成人与专业人员则可能提供更多资源。

Peers can listen and accompany; adults and professionals may offer further resources.

12　这些支持的职责不同，不必全部压在一个人身上。

These roles differ and need not rest on one person's shoulders.

13　因此，求助可以从一个具体困难开始，而不是先给自己诊断。

Help-seeking can begin with a specific difficulty rather than a self-diagnosis.

14　说清发生了什么，以及它怎样影响学习、睡眠或日常生活。

Describe what happened and how it affects learning, sleep or everyday life.

15　再找一位可信任、能够回应的成人一起商量。

Then discuss it with a trusted adult who can respond.

16　如果第一条路径不合适，可以继续寻找其他可靠支持。

If the first path is unsuitable, look for another reliable source of support.

17　支持别人时，也先问对方需要倾听、陪伴，还是实际帮助。

When supporting someone, ask whether they need listening, company or practical help.

18　在能力范围内答应一件具体的事，比承诺解决一切更可靠。

A specific, feasible promise is more reliable than promising to solve everything.

19　如果困扰持续影响生活，或涉及安全，需要连接更多帮助。

Persistent disruption or safety concerns call for further support.

20　这不是要求同伴独自评估或处理所有严重情况。

Peers are not expected to assess or handle every serious situation alone.

21　依恋研究与求助建议来自不同材料，不能当成同一个实验。

Attachment research and help-seeking guidance are different sources, not one experiment.

22　它们在这支片里共同回答的是：支持与自主是否一定冲突。

Together, they address whether support and autonomy must always conflict.

23　答案是：支持可以提供行动的条件，自主也可以包含选择求助。

Support can provide conditions for action, and autonomy can include choosing to seek help.

24　你不必独自承担一切，才算认真参与自己的生活。

You need not carry everything alone to take an active part in your life.

## communication-gap

01　你觉得“很简单”，对方为什么还是做错？

Why can someone still struggle with what seems simple to you?

02　想象你让新同学操作面板，使装置回到起点。

Imagine asking a newcomer to use a control panel to return a device to its starting point.

03　他按下圆形，装置却没有回到起点。

He presses the circle, but the device does not return to the start.

04　“他不认真”则是对原因的解释，还不是已经证实的事实。

Calling him careless is an explanation, not an established fact.

05　归因，就是我们怎样为一个行为寻找原因。

Attribution concerns how we explain the causes of behavior.

06　原因可以涉及个人，也可以涉及说明、界面和现场情境。

Causes may involve the person, the instructions, the interface or the situation.

07　要判断哪一种解释合适，先补上缺的信息。

To judge which explanation fits, first fill in missing information.

08　其中一种可能，是你与新手使用的信息并不相同。

One possibility is that you and the newcomer have different information.

09　你知道符号的含义，新手却只看到一个圆和一个三角。

You know the symbols' meanings; the newcomer sees only a circle and a triangle.

10　知识诅咒研究发现，已知信息可能影响对少信息者判断的预测。

Curse-of-knowledge studies found that known information can affect predictions about less-informed people.

11　用这个提醒检查：说明里省略了哪些新手不知道的步骤？

Use this finding as a prompt: which steps did the instructions omit that a newcomer may not know?

12　但它不能证明每次沟通失败，都是信息差造成的。

It does not establish that every communication failure is caused by an information gap.

13　所以，下一步不是更大声重复“很简单”，而是核对对方看到了什么。

The next step is to check what the other person sees, not repeat the instruction louder.

14　你可以先问：“你觉得这两个符号分别代表什么？”

You could ask what the two symbols mean to them.

15　听完以后，用自己的话复述，再让对方确认。

After listening, restate their meaning and let them confirm it.

16　倾听在这里的作用，是检验你的理解，而不是替对方下定义。

Listening checks your understanding instead of defining the other person's experience.

17　接着把说明改成一个可执行动作和一个可观察反馈。

Then give one executable action and one observable sign of its result.

18　例如：“先按三角，再等黄灯亮起。”

For example: press the triangle, then wait for the yellow light.

19　请对方试一次或复述一遍，看还有哪一步没有接上。

Ask them to try or explain it back to see which step remains unclear.

20　如果仍然卡住，再查语言、任务难度或其他实际条件。

If it still fails, check language, task difficulty and other conditions.

21　理解原因并不取消责任，只是让责任讨论有更完整的信息。

Understanding causes does not erase responsibility; it gives the discussion more information.

22　归因提出解释，知识差提示一个可能，倾听则帮助核对。

Attribution proposes causes, knowledge gaps suggest one possibility, and listening helps check it.

23　这三步连起来，才比一个“他就是不认真”的标签更有用。

Together, these steps are more useful than a quick label of carelessness.

Another line of framing research compares certain and risky options.

That differs from rewording an attribute and should not be merged into the same experiment.

Of every 100, 90 receive it and 10 do not.

Return numbers to their denominators and wording to its conditions.

24　别只问“听起来怎样”，也问“完整说法是什么”。

Ask not only how it sounds, but what the complete statement is.

## future-cost

01　已经花了这么多，就必须继续吗？

Must you continue simply because you have already spent so much?

02　想象你买了一个不能退款的活动名额，去了却发现不合适。

Imagine buying a nonrefundable place at an activity that turns out unsuitable.

03　留下的理由，可能是活动接下来有价值。

A reason to stay may be that the remaining activity is valuable.

04　也可能只是觉得，之前的钱和时间不能白花。

Or it may only feel wrong to waste the money and time already spent.

05　这两种理由，面对的时间方向并不相同。

These reasons face different directions in time.

06　无法收回、已经付出的成本，叫作沉没成本。

An incurred cost that cannot be recovered is a sunk cost.

07　它已经发生，不会因为继续或离开而重新回来。

It has already occurred and will not return simply because you stay or leave.

08　但未来的时间、收益和代价，仍会随选择变化。

Future time, benefits and costs can still change with your decision.

09　1985年，Arkes与Blumer研究了已有投入怎样影响后续选择。

In 1985, Arkes and Blumer studied how prior investment affected later choices.

10　研究中的一种倾向，是让已付出但不可回收的投入影响继续决定。

A tendency studied was letting unrecoverable investment influence a decision to continue.

11　理解这点，不等于认定所有坚持都错。

Understanding this does not make every act of persistence wrong.

12　因为学习到的技能、承诺和转换成本，可能仍影响未来。

Skills, commitments and switching costs may still matter for the future.

13　因此，先做一次分栏：过去已无法收回的，放在一边。

First separate what is already unrecoverable from the rest.

14　从现在开始仍会改变的成本与收益，放在另一边。

Place costs and benefits that can still change from now on in another column.

15　换一种情况，能退回的那部分钱，就不属于沉没成本。

In another case, any refundable portion would not be a sunk cost.

16　如果退出会带来新的责任或损失，也要放进未来一栏。

New responsibilities or losses from leaving also belong in the future column.

17　接着比较：继续以后，还会得到什么、付出什么？

Then compare what continuing will bring and what it will cost.

18　换一条路，又会发生什么？

What would happen if you took another path?

19　两个选择都要用面向未来的理由来说明。

Explain both choices with reasons that concern the future.

20　这种整理帮助你看清理由，再结合具体情况作决定。

This method clarifies the reasons so that you can decide in light of your circumstances.

21　珍惜过去的努力，与调整未来的方向，可以同时成立。

Valuing past effort and changing future direction can coexist.

22　停下可能合理，坚持也可能合理，要看仍然有效的理由。

Stopping or persisting may be reasonable, depending on the reasons that still apply.

23　真正要分开的，是过去的牵挂与未来的比较。

Separate attachment to past investment from comparison of future options.

24　让下一步回答“接下来值得吗”，而不只回答“以前花了多少”。

Let the next step answer what is worthwhile from now on, not only what was spent before.

## research-chain

01　一个研究结果，凭什么值得相信？

What makes a research result worth trusting?

02　设想连续几天，只在午后的球场旁记录冰淇淋销量和树荫人数。

Imagine recording ice-cream sales and people in the shade beside the court each afternoon for several days.

This is an invented example, not an actual dataset.

A conclusion should first state where, when and whom the records cover.

After clarifying that scope, compare the two counts by day.

If days with higher sales also have more people in the shade, we observe a positive correlation.

Correlation alone does not establish causal direction or cause.

Perhaps hotter weather affects both ice-cream purchases and the search for shade.

18　如果结果相近，也不自动说明先前的因果猜想已经被证明。

Similar results still do not automatically prove the proposed causal explanation.

Repeated correlational studies mainly add information about the relationship's robustness.

20　因果判断仍要看设计能否排查竞争解释。

Causal inference still depends on a design that can address competing explanations.
